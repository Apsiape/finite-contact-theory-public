# Admissibility Calculus

Status: public instrument scaffold.

The Admissibility Calculus is the public instrument for deciding what the
theory is allowed to say.

It is not a second competing theory. It is the audit layer: a claim enters the
public theory only when its generation rule, transformation rule, failure rule,
scope, and evidence class are stated.

## Function

The calculus sorts claims into public classes:

- what is forced by finite contact;
- what is recovered from known mathematics or physics;
- what is an extension that diverges from the incumbent — split into an
  unconditional theorem and a conditional, experiment-open, bridge-premise-gated
  prediction (introduced by Chapter 10; tested by internal rigor and a sharp
  observable divergence, not by reduction to the incumbent);
- what is model-scope only;
- what is received or admitted as overlay;
- what is open;
- what has been withdrawn.

## Admission Test

A claim is public-admissible only if it supplies:

1. a finite construction or rule;
2. the object or observable it produces;
3. the transformations under which it is invariant or changes;
4. a discriminating control or must-fail case where applicable;
5. a failure condition;
6. a status label;
7. a source or verification path.

## Core Warning

The calculus must not be used to launder old words into new symbols.

If a term such as `world`, `observer`, `receiver`, `time`, `energy`,
`probability`, `quantum`, or `spacetime` appears, it inherits the burden of the
structure it normally carries. The public text must say whether the term is:

- recovered;
- dissolved;
- redefined;
- admitted as an overlay;
- left open.

## Relation To The Private Lineage

The private research lineage currently provides the strongest public narrative spine. The
Admissibility Calculus provides the public discipline that keeps that narrative
from outrunning its evidence.

Public extraction rule:

> The private research corpus supplies the body of the public account; the calculus supplies the
> skeleton, claim labels, and immune system.
