## 0. What this chapter claims

Chapter 16 priced irreversibility: distinction is never destroyed
unpaid. This chapter shows that price is not one result but the
program's *load-bearing frame*: the capability atlas (Ch14), the
law/present Cut (Ch3), the identifiability and debt calculus (Ch3), the
arrow (Ch5), and the measurement relocation (Ch14) are faces of the one
ledger, with one shared boundary — and physical constants acquire a
structural definition as the ledger's third column. The components that
are classical are cited as classical (§7); what is claimed is the
organization: **one framework, four pillars, one boundary.**

## 1. The price field on the atlas (Section A of the verifier)

Chapter 14's atlas classified floors by what they can carry. The ledger
adds *who pays*:

- **Cell 00 (lattice/coagulation): PAID.** The settled state of a
  constraint floor is a paid quotient — order-independence (confluence)
  is *bought* (max basin 25 of 64 domain-states in the shipped model);
  a coagulation floor pays its entire state space to its arrow.
- **Cell 10 (reversible): FLAT.** Permutation acts can neither pay nor
  mint.
- **Cell 01 (creation): CREDIT.** Injective creation mints distinction
  at zero cumulative price — the ledger's source term.
- **Cell 11 (marks): MIXED — and this is the load-bearing exact fact.**
  On the relocation model (E = 2T over L = A₄, Chapter 14): every
  contact act is a permutation, so *history is written free*; the law
  quotient has maxfiber exactly 2, uniformly — **the price of
  lawfulness is exactly one bit, and it is the retained central bit of
  Chapter 13.** The minimal record alphabet restoring injectivity is
  2 = maxfiber, so Chapter 14's chance→certainty registration promotion
  is precisely a 1-bit ledger transaction: the record pays back what
  the quotient destroyed.

One sentence the atlas could not say before: **the marks cell is the
only floor whose ledger balances internally — creation credit funds
quotient debt — the only floor that can afford its own measurements.**

## 2. The funding identity: the forced fork, and mint = bill (Section B)

What funds the credit? Exhaustively, on one-use floors:

- **All minted distinction is consumption-choice distinction** — the
  fork is the only source term (the mint is faithful; every contact
  step is injective).
- **The forced fork.** Deterministic multi-step one-use floors do not
  exist: any floor admitting a two-step history already has at least
  two admissible pairs at step one (exhaustive: 4752 floors). The
  lemma itself is an instance of the persistence/diamond property of
  consume-only transition systems, standard in concurrency theory
  (Winskel; Nielsen–Plotkin–Winskel; conflict-free nets — cited, §7).
  What the program adds is the *use*: the fork-staging half of its
  central law ("the floor stages a fork…") is thereby demoted from
  axiom to theorem at this model scope. The no-selector half remains
  the law's irreducible content — the theory's genuine postulate is
  now exactly located.
- **Mint = bill, class by class.** The admissible orderings of each
  reachable content class equal exactly the fiber of the
  order-forgetting quotient over that class. The combinatorial identity
  is definitional in trace theory (Mazurkiewicz traces / Cartier–Foata;
  the query-side is the sorting-under-partial-information bound of
  Kahn–Kim — cited, §7); the accounting reading — the credit the fork
  mints forward equals the debt the content presentation pays backward,
  per class, so waste is possible and theft impossible in both
  directions — is the chapter's commentary on a known identity.

## 3. The Cut, derived and flow-typed (Section C)

Chapter 3 published the Cut as a regularity: laws identify in O(1),
presents in Θ(N). The ledger explains it and locates its boundary:

- **Derived:** the present is expensive because it is *minted* — its
  candidate set is the mint itself (exact reachable counts 1, 45, 1260,
  18900, 113400 in the shipped model), so identification cost grows as
  the log of a geometric mint. (The law side at this model scope is
  stipulative — a declared finite rule family; Chapter 3's U-statistic
  theorem is the published law-side result.)
- **Flow-typed:** paid cells *destroy* the Cut at their terminals —
  settlement collapses the candidate set (64 → 16; 6 → 4 queries), and
  coagulation's terminal present costs exactly zero to identify. Flat
  cells never grow one (orbits saturate at |G|).

> **The Cut lives exactly on cells with credit flow: the law/present
> asymmetry is the signature of injective growth — the Cut is the
> signature of the mint.**

The two-part-code literature (algorithmic statistics: Gács–Tromp–
Vitányi; Vereshchagin–Vitányi; computational mechanics: Crutchfield —
cited, §7) supplies the model/data decomposition this refines; the
blind sweep found no prior statement of the identification-cost
trichotomy as a necessary-and-sufficient signature of growth type.

## 5. The three-column Cut: constants as registrations (Section E)

A structural definition, exact at model scope: **a constant is a
fork-registration that the law action inherits.** On a rule family
whose members are exchanged by a relabeling symmetry (two edge-disjoint
isomorphic tolerances in the shipped model), the selecting parameter θ
is:

- **underived** — no equivariant selector exists at the level of rules
  (Chapter 3's selector theorem, one level up);
- **O(1)-readable from every window** — because it parameterizes the
  monoid every event obeys (every single contact reveals it);
- **paid once, replicated free** — one bit at the genesis fork, then
  replication number R = 1 across all windows, versus decaying
  replication for present bits.

So the Cut has three columns: **law = derivable, O(1) · constant =
underived, O(1) · present = underived, Θ(N).** This *dissolves* rather
than names a puzzle: omnipresence ("why are constants the same
everywhere?") is not a mystery but the definition of column two.
Informal ancestors are real and cited (§7): frozen accidents
(Gell-Mann), law without law (Wheeler; Müller's algorithmic version),
cosmological natural selection (Smolin), environmental selection
(Weinberg). The blind sweep found no prior *operational* three-property
definition separating constants from both laws and states by
identification cost and replication. Column-two objects already in this
program: Chapter 13's orientation bit, Chapter 9's world-phase
selection, and the research corpus's retrodicted constants (cited).

Honesty note carried from the research record: the replication *decay
gradient* for present bits is established as a separation (R = 1 vs
R < 1), not exhibited as a gradient, in the shipped model.

## 6. The two bills: the ledger's external limit (Section F; numeric
## discovery grade, labeled)

Does the ledger price meet measured dissipation? **No — and the
failure is the finding.** Using the standard housekeeping/excess
decomposition (Hatano–Sasa; Speck–Seifert — cited):

- A driven floor's steady-state dissipation is *housekeeping* — cycle
  driving with zero contraction (EP = 0.0327 in the shipped model with
  the system entropy rate at zero; the structure-blind point has
  detailed balance and EP = 0). It carries no logical merging at all.
- The ledger price floors the *excess* bill only: every erasure
  protocol dissipates at least the entropy contraction, whose clean
  limit is ln 2 = the 2-merge price in nats (Landauer's face —
  RECOVERY, labeled; Maroney and the logical-vs-thermodynamic
  reversibility literature cited, §7).
- The corners tune independently: driving with zero merges (EP > 0,
  price 0); cycle-free erasure (excess > 0, housekeeping 0).

> **The floor pays two independent bills — exploration (housekeeping,
> per unit time) and selection (the ledger price, per merge). The
> ledger prices selection and only selection.**

Per the sweep: the decomposition and the orthogonality lore are an
organizing restatement of known thermodynamics of computation
(Maroney; Kolchinsky–Wolpert); the sliver claimed is the
identification of the *combinatorial* merge price of Chapter 16 as the
excess floor.
