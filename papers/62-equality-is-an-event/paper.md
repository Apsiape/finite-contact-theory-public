## 1. The root, and the one-line derivation of tolerance

### 1.1 The root

State it plainly, as the sprint did.

> **R (equality is an event).** Nothing is ever "the same" — as another, or as
> itself again — except by a further occurrence that is spent in the
> identifying and leaves a mark. **F (finitude):** only finitely many
> identifications have ever been paid.

This is an EXTENSION / SYNTHESIS, and the incumbent it is adjudicated against
is named and classical: the assumption, nearly universal and rarely written
down, that identity is *free* (costs nothing to assert), *transitive* (chains
of sameness compose for free), and *eternally reusable* (a thing may be
identified with itself any number of times at no charge). R denies exactly the
freeness; F denies the completedness. The claim is not that this is true of
nature — that would be a category error against our own labels. The claim is
that it is a better-organized bottom for *this program* than the primitives we
had, because it derives several of them. We make that case by derivation, not
by assertion, starting with the sharpest one.

### 1.2 Tolerance = equality minus free transitivity

The program's floor has always run on a *tolerance* relation `a ~ b`:
reflexive (`a ~ a`), symmetric (`a ~ b ⇒ b ~ a`), but pointedly NOT transitive
(`a ~ b` and `b ~ c` do not give `a ~ c` for free). We posited that. It is the
single structural choice from which the presheaf-not-sheaf character of the
whole theory follows — the refusal of "glue for free."

Under R it is a one-line consequence, and this is the chapter's first THEOREM
(model scope):

- **Reflexivity** is the cost of a *re*-identification: to have `a ~ a` at all
  is to have paid one occurrence identifying `a` with itself. The relation is
  reflexive exactly because that occurrence is admitted.
- **Symmetry** holds because the identifying occurrence is a single mark
  relating the two, with no built-in direction: the act that pays `a ~ b` is
  the same act that pays `b ~ a`.
- **Non-transitivity** is the whole content of R. `a ~ b` is one paid
  occurrence; `b ~ c` is another; `a ~ c` would be a *third*, and R says it is
  never free. Transitivity is precisely the assumption that the third
  identification comes gratis from the first two. Deny free identity and you
  deny exactly transitivity, keeping reflexivity and symmetry — which is the
  definition of a tolerance relation.

So **tolerance = equality with free transitivity removed.** An equivalence
relation is what a tolerance becomes if you grant free transitivity — the
classical free-identity assumption in relational form. The floor axiom we
posited is the shadow R casts once you refuse that grant. Nothing here is
verified by the census script (it is a proof, not a computation); it is stated
as a THEOREM at model scope and it is the reason the root is worth taking
seriously rather than admiring.

Two immediate corollaries in the same key, carried at model scope: *marks* are
pure occurrences with no persistent self-identity (they cannot re-identify
across time for free); *one-use* is the denial of free self-identification of
a mark with its later self; *receipts* are the identifying act itself seen as
a new mark; presheaf-not-sheaf is the sheaf axiom's "glue for free" refused at
the level of gluing. Each of these was a floor posit. Each is R applied at a
different scope. The claim "R explains more with fewer posits" is adjudicable
in exactly this list.

## 2. The reception census: exactly-once is a 2-coloring

### 2.1 The construction

R makes a census possible without smuggling in a selector. The floor forces no
selector, so counting *which* branch a fork takes would measure the
simulator's resolver, not the floor. Instead we count a gauge-free observable:
over a horizon of staged forks that share lineage under one-use, how many joint
branch-strings are structurally realizable (`N`) versus the naive product
`K = 2^m`? The log-ratio `Λ = ln(K/N)` is the amount of joint outcome that
one-use forbids. No selector is invoked; we count realizable configurations.

The model is the natural one. Give each of `m` binary forks a bit `b_i` (did
this fork consume its shared mark). A mark shared between forks `i` and `j`,
under one-use *exactly once* — floor conservation laws L1 (no unreceipted
erasure) and L2 (no duplication) together, so the mark is accounted exactly
once — imposes `b_i ≠ b_j`. That is a proper-2-coloring edge. The
lineage-sharing topology is a graph on the forks, and:

> **THEOREM (model scope).** One-use exactly-once realizability = proper
> 2-colorability = bipartiteness. `N` = number of proper 2-colorings = `2^c`
> if the graph is bipartite (`c` connected components), else `0`.

Verified by the shipped script `verify_62_equality.py`, part (a): two
independent engines — explicit enumeration of all `2^m` assignments, and a
components-and-bipartiteness check — agree on every topology.

### 2.2 The table

| topology | `K` | `N` | `Λ = ln(K/N)` | reading |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 forks, 1 shared mark | 4 | 2 | ln 2 ≈ 0.693 | parity `a ⊕ b = 1` |
| 3 forks, path (tree) | 8 | 2 | 1.386 | bipartite, `N > 0` |
| **3-cycle (odd)** | 8 | **0** | **∞** | every pair realizable, whole impossible |
| 4-cycle (even) | 16 | 2 | 2.079 | bipartite, Bell-local |
| **5-cycle (odd)** | 32 | **0** | **∞** | globally unrealizable |
| 6-cycle (even) | 64 | 2 | 3.466 | bipartite, Bell-local |
| K4 complete | 16 | 0 | ∞ | odd triangles inside |

The pattern is exactly the graph theory: even cycles and trees are bipartite
and realizable; odd cycles are not 2-colorable and give `N = 0`, a total
obstruction in which every proper subset (every pair) is realizable while the
whole is not. `Λ` moves through a discrete ladder of multiples of `ln 2` and
never a continuum — a small model-scope confirmation that the received region
here is granular, not continuous. All of this is reproduced in exact arithmetic
by the script.

## 3. The correction, shipped loudly

Here is where we got the *meaning* wrong, and it is the most useful part of the
chapter.

### 3.1 What we claimed, and the kill

We claimed the odd-cycle obstruction WAS the non-amalgamable triad that sits
under the program's one registered divergence candidate (a negative-Gram
tritter quantity) — that "the non-amalgamable triad is a counting theorem of
one-use." Three external reviewers, given only the census summary, converged
on the same correction. It is **WITHDRAWN**, and verified as withdrawn by the
script, part (b). There are three distinct objects, and we had conflated them:

- **(A) `a ⊕ b ⊕ c = 0` — classical conservation.** Every pair of coordinates
  realizes all four combinations (the pairwise marginals are *complete*), only
  four of eight triples occur, AND a positive global section exists (the
  uniform distribution on the four triples is itself a valid joint
  distribution). Pairwise-complete, globally consistent, classical.
- **(B) the odd 2-coloring cycle** (`a ≠ b`, `b ≠ c`, `c ≠ a`) — **the object
  our census actually hit.** Each edge-pair realizes only two of four
  combinations, so the pairs are *constrained*, not complete; and the whole is
  frustrated, `N = 0`. Pairwise-constrained and globally frustrated.
- **(C) the Peres–Mermin square — genuine contextuality.** Every context (a
  row or column of three commuting two-qubit observables) is individually
  satisfiable and the pairwise data is *complete*, yet there is no global `±1`
  assignment. Pairwise-complete, no global section.

The distinction that kills the overclaim: (B) is pairwise-*constrained*, while
(C) is pairwise-*complete*. Our object was (B); the tritter-class object is
(C). They are not the same, and we no longer say they are. This is stated as a
**THEOREM (model scope)** — the three objects have the three verified
signatures — carrying an attached WITHDRAWN on the earlier identification.

### 3.2 The verification of the taxonomy

The script, part (b), certifies each object exactly:

- (A): all three pairwise marginals of `a ⊕ b ⊕ c = 0` are complete (4/4
  combinations), and an explicit positive global distribution is exhibited and
  checked consistent.
- (B): each of the three edges of the odd 2-coloring cycle realizes exactly two
  of four combinations (constrained), every single edge is individually
  realizable, and the whole has `N = 0`.
- (C): the Peres–Mermin square is built from exact two-qubit Pauli observables
  over the Gaussian integers; every row and column product is computed and
  found to be exactly `±I`; the three row-products multiply to `+1` and the
  three column-products to `−1`. Since a hypothetical global `±1` assignment
  would have to give the *same* product of the nine values whether grouped by
  rows or by columns, `+1 ≠ −1` is a contradiction: no global assignment
  exists. This is the standard state-independent contextuality argument (Peres
  1990; Mermin 1990), reconstructed here in exact integer arithmetic to hold
  (B) and (C) apart mechanically.

### 3.3 What the corrected result is, honestly

The odd-cycle contextuality that (C) exhibits is known — it is Kochen–Specker
class (Kochen and Specker 1967), observed, and consistent with the empirical
tier the program holds sacred. Object (B)'s frustration is classical graph
frustration. Neither is a new empirical door, and we claim none. The corrected
contribution is a **RECOVERY with a placement**: within the model, the
one-use conservation laws L1 + L2 at an odd cycle produce object (B) as a
counting fact — low novelty, and adjudicable against the exclusivity-principle
framework (Cabello–Severini–Winter 2014) in that they *posit* exclusivity
while the floor *derives* the two-coloring constraint from its own two
conservation laws. What we do NOT get from unordered one-use counting is (C);
whatever supplies the sign that turns frustration into genuine contextuality
is not in the exclusion structure alone, and locating it is flagged OPEN. The
census stands as computed; only its interpretation was corrected.

## 4. The exact twins

The same reading law that generates the census generates, at depth two, a
different and sharper object.

### 4.1 The construction and the fact

Run the floor's own genesis successor on a small cycle arena and read off, at
depth two, the reading-law channels: each channel is keyed by its sequence of
contact events and its final canonical class, and carries an amplitude
`Σ i^{dev} · w` over its paths, where `dev` is the deviation the successor
records and `w` is the path weight. Arrange the amplitudes into a table with
one row per canonical class and one column per event-context. Then:

> **THEOREM (model scope, depth 2).** For the C5 and C6 cycle arenas, the
> depth-2 class-amplitude table is RANK-DEFICIENT: `det G = 0` exactly. C4 is
> full rank (interior). For C5 the rank is 4 of 5; for C6 it is 5 of 6. In both
> cases the null vector is a single canonical class minus another, coefficients
> `±1` exactly: **two distinct canonical classes carrying identical amplitude
> vectors across every event-context.** Exact twins.

Verified by the shipped script, part (c). The engine is reimplemented
self-contained in the script — neighbor sets, the genesis successor, the
canonical-class map under vertex relabeling, and exact Gaussian-rational Gauss
elimination — and the amplitude table is recomputed from first principles, not
hardcoded. C5 returns rank 4 with null `class[0] − class[2]`; C6 returns rank 5
with the same shape. The determinant is exactly zero because the arithmetic is
exact (fractions of Gaussian integers), so this is a genuine degeneracy, not a
rounding coincidence.

### 4.2 The coat-universality theorem

The twins are not an artifact of a coarse readout, and this is worth stating as
its own model-scope theorem:

> **THEOREM (model scope, depth 2, coat-universality).** No deviation-dependent
> reading law can split the twins or give them opposite signs at depth two. The
> twin classes carry identical channel data — the same paths, the same
> deviations, the same weights, in every event-context — so any reading law
> that is a function of that channel data (the coat `i^{dev}`, or any other
> function of the recorded deviations and weights) assigns them identical
> values. Distinguishing the twins requires channel data the depth-2 table does
> not contain.

This is why the twins are the program's central law made exact. "The floor
stages a fork and forces no selector" says the floor presents alternatives it
cannot itself choose between. The twins are two structurally distinct outcomes
that the entire depth-2 reading law is provably blind to: staged as distinct,
indistinguishable to any deviation-dependent instrument at that depth. The fork
is exhibited, and the impossibility of a reading-law selector for it is a
theorem rather than a slogan.

### 4.3 Scope fences

We fence this precisely. It is depth-2: deeper reading may split the twins, and
whether depth 3 does is OPEN and un-run. It is a twin *degeneracy* — two
classes with equal vectors — not a many-class cancellation. And it is not a
divergence claim: the table sits exactly on the boundary of the
positive-semidefinite cone (marginally amalgamable, one perturbation from
non-positive), which makes the location precise but asserts no disagreement
with nature. A holonomy that gave the twin pair opposite signs in even one
context would tip the determinant negative immediately — zero margin to cross —
but the floor is not shown to force any such holonomy, and we claim none. The
phenomenon is a model-scope structural fact with a named next question.

## 5. The falsifiability line: a process rule born from a retraction

The twin result exists because an earlier run in the same campaign was retracted
and the retraction is part of the public record.

That run claimed to measure the sign structure of the floor's reading law by
building a class-Gram matrix and checking its signature. The matrix was built
as `G = A · A†`. Such a matrix is positive-semidefinite *by construction*: it
cannot have a negative eigenvalue no matter what the engine does. The claimed
finding — "signature `(p, 0)` across all arenas," i.e. no negative directions —
was therefore guaranteed before the engine ran. It measured its own
construction. That claim is **WITHDRAWN**, printed here where it stood.

What survived the retraction is exactly the twins: the *rank* of `A · A†` is
not tautological — it measures linear dependence of the amplitude vectors — and
recomputed in exact arithmetic it gave the degeneracy of §4. The tautological
observable (positivity) died; the honest observable (rank) was the interesting
one all along.

The discipline banked from this is a standing process rule, and we state it as
the chapter's methodological deliverable:

> **The falsifiability-line rule.** No instrument ships without a written line
> stating what result would falsify its hypothesis. A construction of the form
> `A · A†` has no such line for positivity — it can only ever return a positive
> answer — so it can never test positivity; it can only test rank. Every
> quantity reported must have an outcome that would have refuted the claim.

The shipped script obeys its own rule: it ends with an explicit falsifiability
note enumerating, per part, the result that would break each claim — engine
disagreement or a 2-colorable odd cycle for (a); a pairwise-complete odd cycle
or an agreeing Peres–Mermin parity for (b); a full-rank C5/C6 table or a
non-`±1`, many-class null for (c) — and it tests rank, never positivity, for
precisely the reason above.

## 6. Adjacent mathematics, and what is new

The nearest established mathematics is proof-relevant equality. In homotopy
type theory the identity type is not a bare proposition: a proof that `a = b`
is a genuine object, paths compose and have inverses, and equalities between
equalities form a tower (Hofmann and Streicher 1998, groupoid model of type
theory; the Univalent Foundations Program's *Homotopy Type Theory* book,
2013). That `a = b` carries a *witness* rather than a truth value is exactly the
spirit of "equality is an event."

The distinction we draw, and the only thing we mark as new here, is precise and
narrow. In homotopy type theory the identity witnesses are *freely reusable*: a
path may be traversed any number of times, composed with itself, inverted, and
substituted without cost — the identity type is a structure, not a resource.
Our addition, under R + F, is to make the witness **resource-sensitive**:
one-use, spent in the identifying, recorded as a receipt, finite in supply. No
existing substructural type theory places the resource discipline on the
identity type itself — linear and affine type theories meter the *terms*, not
the equality witnesses between them. Grading the equality witness as a consumed
resource is the specific move, and we carry it as a SYNTHESIS to be adjudicated,
not a theorem. The classical mathematics it borrows (proof-relevant equality)
is RECOVERY, cited as such; the resource discipline on the witness is the
delta.
