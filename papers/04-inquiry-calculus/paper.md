# Chapter 4 — Questions as Operators: the Inquiry Calculus and a Second Law of Asking

Status: frozen at `v0.4.0`. Claim rows: FCT-30..FCT-33. Theorem rows:
T-22..T-25. Shipped verification: `inquiry_calculus.py`.

## Ceiling for this chapter

This chapter publishes a finite calculus of *asking*: questions act as
operators on finite epistemic objects; the operator algebra is
noncommutative in general and its commutative sector is exactly the
count-only ("Boolean shadow") sector; the expected cost of any admissible
question protocol decomposes exactly as irreducible entropy plus mismatch
plus slack (`EC = H + KL + O`, each term nonnegative — a second law of
asking); adaptive questioning strictly beats fixed questioning on explicit
witnesses; and self-reference is lawful under grading while paradox is
exactly type collapse. Every statement is a finite, machine-checkable
theorem or an explicit witness at the stated scope. The chapter does not
claim that nature's measurements *are* these operators; the quantum-facing
reading is `cited` context, not a claim.

## 1. Questions as residual operators (T-22)

Let `Sigma` be a finite alphabet of elementary askable distinctions and let
an *epistemic object* be a finitely supported weight function `S` on words
`Sigma*` (a formal series: which ordered histories of answers remain live,
with what weight). Asking `a` transforms `S` into its *residual*
`(a^-1 S)(w) = S(aw)` — the epistemic object conditioned on having received
answer-event `a` first.

**Theorem (the asking algebra).** Residuals compose contravariantly:
`u^-1 (v^-1 S) = (vu)^-1 S` for all words `u, v`. The algebra generated by
`{a^-1 : a in Sigma}` is noncommutative in general — explicit witnesses
with `a^-1 b^-1 S != b^-1 a^-1 S` are shipped — and on *count-only* series
(those depending only on the multiset of letters, not their order) all
residuals commute. Conversely, order-sensitivity of the series forces
noncommutativity of some pair.

The commutative sector is the chapter's *Boolean shadow*: when only counts
matter, asking has a classical logic; asking becomes order-sensitive
exactly when histories carry more than their letter counts. The shipped
script verifies the composition law on randomized series, exhibits the
noncommutativity witness, and verifies the count-only biconditional
exhaustively at small scope.

## 2. The second law of asking (T-23)

Let a *protocol* be a finite binary decision tree identifying outcomes
`x` with probabilities `p_x` at leaf depths `d_x`, so the Kraft weight
`Z = sum_x 2^(-d_x) <= 1`. Define the protocol's implicit belief
`q_x = 2^(-d_x) / Z`.

**Theorem (exact cost decomposition).** The expected cost obeys, exactly,

```
EC  =  sum_x p_x d_x  =  H(p) + KL(p || q) + O,      O = -log2 Z >= 0,
```

with `H` the Shannon entropy and `KL` the relative entropy. Hence
`EC >= H(p)` always, with equality iff the protocol's implicit belief
matches the source dyadically (`KL = 0`) and the tree is full (`O = 0`).

Reading: the cost of asking splits into three nonnegative parts — the
*irreducible* entropy of what is being identified, the *mismatch* between
what the asker implicitly believes and what is the case, and pure *slack*
(unused resolving power). Nothing identifies below `H`; this is the second
law of asking. The script verifies the identity to `1e-12` on randomized
trees and sources, verifies `Z <= 1` in exact rational arithmetic, and
witnesses each equality condition separately.

The `ceil(log2 m)` selector debt of chapter 3 is the worst-case corner of
this law (uniform `p`, exact identification); T-23 is its
distribution-weighted refinement.

## 3. Adaptivity interest (T-24)

A *fixed* interrogation asks a predetermined sequence of binary questions;
an *adaptive* one lets each question depend on earlier answers. The
admitted question *family* is part of the model: the shipped witness uses
threshold questions (`is x < t?`) on an ordered four-outcome space —
notably, with *unrestricted* subset questions and early stopping, fixed
interrogation matches adaptive at this size, so the interest is a property
of asking under a constrained repertoire, which is the physically honest
case.

**Theorem (nonnegative interest, strict witness).** For every finite
outcome space, source, and admitted family, the optimal adaptive expected
cost is at most the optimal fixed expected cost
(`J = EC_fixed* - EC_adaptive* >= 0`, by inclusion of strategy spaces), and
there are explicit sources where the inequality is strict — the shipped
witness is the uniform source under thresholds: adaptive `2.0` vs best
fixed `2.25`. The script computes both optima by exhaustive search.

Adaptivity is therefore a genuine resource of inquiry, with a measurable
interest rate — not a bookkeeping convenience.

## 4. Self-question typing: paradox is type collapse (T-25)

Within the calculus a question may be about the asker's own answers,
provided levels are graded: an answer at grade `n` may inform a question
at grade `n+1`. **Theorem (witnesses).** Over the two-element answer space,
the ungraded self-equation `x = x` has exactly two solutions and the
ungraded `x = 1 - x` has none; the *graded* recursion `x_{n+1} = 1 - x_n`
has exactly two lawful orbits and no contradiction. Paradox is what remains
when the grade that separates a live asking from its recorded answer is
collapsed; with the grade kept, self-reference is lawful and productive.
(Shipped by exhaustion; the general guarded-recursion framing is `cited`.)

## 5. What this chapter does not claim

- It does not claim quantum measurement *is* the residual calculus; the
  structural rhyme (noncommutative asking with a commutative classical
  shadow) is `cited` program context.
- The cost decomposition is proved for binary decision-tree protocols; more
  general answer alphabets rescale the logarithm and are not shipped.
- The adaptivity witness is exhaustive small-scope; asymptotic rates are
  not claimed.

## Falsifiers at this chapter's scope

Mechanical: a series violating the residual composition law; a count-only
series with noncommuting residuals (or an order-sensitive one with all
residuals commuting at the tested horizon); a protocol tree with
`EC - H - KL - O != 0` or `Z > 1`; an adaptive optimum exceeding a fixed
optimum; a solution to ungraded `x = 1 - x`. The shipped script searches
for each and must find none.
