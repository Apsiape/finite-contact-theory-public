## 1. What this chapter is

Chapters 1–6 are six load-bearing walls. This chapter is the map: what the
walls hold up, how they connect, and what the program is actually claiming
at the level above any single theorem. It introduces **no new theorems**.
Its three claim rows are *organizing schemas* — honest labels for the
patterns that recur across the shipped stack — and each schema names which
shipped rows instantiate it and which parts remain cited or open.

## 2. The two published axes

**The quantum-facing axis** (chapters 1–2). From a finite floor of one-use
marks: coherent counting (T-03), one-receiver gluing (T-10), rational Born
weights as counting pushed through gluing (T-04), the CHSH/Pell boundary

grammar with the complementarity angle `t = k/n` (T-06/T-07), the
binary-Bell quantum boundary as a floor theorem (T-08), and — in standard
quantum-foundations language — the behavior-conditioned capacity and the
exact strict preparation gap (T-14..T-17).

**The finite-epistemics axis** (chapters 3–6). What identification *is* and
costs: the waist biconditional, the `ceil(log2 m)` selector debt, the
no-equivariant-selector theorem, continuation sufficiency (T-18..T-21);
what asking *is* and costs: the noncommutative residual algebra with its
Boolean shadow, the exact second law `EC = H + KL + O`, the adaptivity
interest, paradox as type collapse (T-22..T-25); what those costs force and
forbid in time-like structure: torsor time, the helix covering, the ledger
arrow, lawfulness without foundation (T-26..T-29); and a measured
generative floor with three fenced measurements and an
independent-implementation agreement (T-30..T-32).

The axes meet: the floor of chapters 1–2 is a generative dynamics whose
identification structure is exactly what chapters 3–4 price, and whose
measured phenomenology (chapter 6) exhibits the delayed individuation and
counted consequence-propagation that the epistemics predicts is *possible*.
The meeting is stated here as architecture, not as a theorem.

## 3. The organizing schemas (the chapter's claim rows)

**FCT-42 — the two-axis public stack.** The index claim: the public
repository contains exactly the scoped rows FCT-01..FCT-41 / T-01..T-32,
organized as above, each runnable or cited as labeled. This row is
falsified by any row that fails its own shipped check or misstates its
scope.

**FCT-43 — the forced/received schema.** Across every published chapter,
what the finite structure *forces* is grammar, signs, order, exact
biconditionals, and exact constants of form (the debt `ceil(log2 m)`, the
decomposition `EC = H + KL + O`, the heap identities, the monodromy);
what it never forces is a *selection* — which alternative, which origin,
which magnitude. Shipped instances: T-18/T-19 (identification vs
selection), T-23 (cost form vs source), T-26 (time structure vs origin).
The program-level generalization — that this split is the deep structure
of physical law — is a `SCHEMA`, cited research posture, not a shipped
theorem, and is stated with that label.

**FCT-44 — the no-selector recurrence.** One obstruction arrives at every
level examined: a symmetry-respecting rule cannot select a point of a
symmetric fiber (T-19, shipped), so any actual selection — an origin of
time (T-26), an actual alternative (T-19's setting), a unit or scale
(cited) — must be *received*, and its record costs at least
`ceil(log2 m)` bits (T-19; refined by T-23; run forward as the arrow in
T-28). The recurrence across the shipped rows is checkable; its
program-level reading (selection as the single received residue of
physics) is `SCHEMA / cited`.

## 4. What would kill it

At row level: any shipped check failing on a clean clone. At schema level:
a derived, symmetry-respecting selector at any examined level (it would
break FCT-44 and T-19's applications at once); an identification scheme
beating the debt bound; an asking protocol identifying below entropy; a
counterexample to a stated biconditional. The correction ledger records
what has already died; the program treats a growing kill-count as the
discipline working.

## 5. Held open, by name

The general quantum selector; `q >= 3` and cross-site interlocking; the
inversion of individuation order at public scale (cited at private scale,
explicitly not claimed publicly); n-scaling laws for the measured floor;
defect radius (cone speed proper); continuum and relativistic structure;
every nature-facing prediction. Nothing in this chapter promotes a held
row.
