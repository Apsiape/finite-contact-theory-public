## 1. The steering radius is one hop

Whether a retention assignment reproduces the world's class is NOT
determined by the participants' own adjacency rows (2 of 32
canonical types are ambiguous) but IS determined perfectly by the
radius-1 ball — rows plus the edges among the neighbors (138 types,
zero violations, exhaustive n=5). A registered rows-suffice lean
died on this engine and is scored. **Classification:** the
rows-vs-ball gap is the neighborhood-reconstruction phenomenon
(shotgun assembly, cited); the exhaustive threshold for this
contact operator is the instance.

## 2. The gossip theorem and the privacy failure

The ball is exactly the union of its members' rows — one round of
neighbor gossip — so the upkeep decision never needs a global map.
The privacy failure is exhibited concretely: two worlds identical in
everything the participants can privately know, where one
neighbor-neighbor edge decides whether the same local choice
preserves or destroys the world. **Classification:** the
round-radius correspondence and the indistinguishability gadget are
the LOCAL model's (Linial cited); the exhibited pair is the
substrate's instance.

## 4. The zero-traffic bootstrap

Under label reuse a mark's row changes only at its OWN contacts, so
private-sector steering data is fresh by construction; maintained
worlds present the same steering problems forever (stationarity);
hence maintenance on the private sector is self-consistent with no
information channel beyond participation itself. The gossip sector,
by contrast, provably cannot run on memory: 96 of 125 simulated

private sector is a silent / zero-communication self-stabilizing
regime (Dijkstra; Dolev–Gouda–Schneider cited — including their
result that NON-silent invariants must check forever, which is
exactly the gossip sector's fate); cache staleness is the standard

## 6. The habitability theorem — with its caution

Combining Chapter 21's observer filter (bounded observers need
bounded-degree worlds) with this release's maintenance ledger: at
tested scope, the worlds that can host bounded observers are worlds
their inhabitants can afford to maintain — habitable =
maintainable. **Classification (the sweep's second priority
candidate, PLAUSIBLY-NEW as a stated equivalence): its conceptual
parents are Conant–Ashby's good-regulator theorem and Ashby's
requisite variety on one side and autopoiesis on the other (all
cited) — frameworks that keep the observer-existence filter and the

THE CAUTION, absorbed from the sweep and stated as part of the

affordable) provably resolve to the same structural root. It is a
convergence theorem about that shared root, not a coincidence of
unrelated filters; readers should weigh it as such.
