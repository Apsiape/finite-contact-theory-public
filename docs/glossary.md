# Glossary

**A living glossary — current through v0.35 (61 chapters).**

This is a plain-language door into the vocabulary a newcomer meets in the
[README](../README.md) and the chapters. Each entry gives a lay gloss first,
then a one-line technical gloss, then a pointer to the chapter or document that
earns the term. These are public shorthand; where a chapter's status label or
scope is narrower than a gloss sounds, the chapter wins. Terms and definitions
evolve with the release — corrections and disputes are welcome at
apsiape@gmail.com.

---

**accessible positivity** — Even when the hidden bookkeeping goes negative, every probability you could actually measure with ordinary optics stays non-negative. Technical: the property that every passive-linear-optical probability is nonnegative even when the underlying class function has a negative eigenvalue — the Chapter-10 theorem `152N + 9|per|^2 - 36|det|^2 >= 0` for every complex `3x3` matrix. ([Chapter 10](../papers/10-negative-gram-holonomy/paper.md))

**admission data** — A choice the theory deliberately leaves open — a knob, interface, or ingredient the rules permit but never fix. Technical: a declared parameter, product, completion, or carrier choice not forced by the current floor, tracked as a received input rather than a theorem. ([public claim register](public-claim-register.md) · [Chapter 56](../papers/56-fixed-point-and-receipts/paper.md))

**aperture** — The limited window through which a finite thing sees a world larger than itself, showing some structure and hiding the rest. Technical: a bounded visible sector or quotient at a stated scope; the release keeps floor-level contact distinct from aperture-level physics. ([Chapter 1](../papers/finite-contact-theory-v0.1.md) · [Chapter 53](../papers/53-escape-and-forks/paper.md))

**bright channel** — An outcome that stays visible because its contributions add up instead of cancelling. Technical: a census channel with surviving (nonzero) amplitude; bright correlation-carrying channels can collapse to a single reveal. ([Chapter 33](../papers/33-the-codes-of-the-coat/paper.md) · [Chapter 37](../papers/37-permission-map/paper.md))

**carrier** — The finite algebraic "alphabet" a world grows in order to record its contacts and combine them. Technical: a finite algebraic grammar grown or admitted at a stated scope — not automatically all of quantum theory. ([Chapter 55](../papers/55-carrier-anatomy/paper.md))

**ceiling** — The single long sentence that fences in everything the current release is allowed to claim. Technical: the live release ceiling, quoted verbatim across the README, the claim register, and the capstone chapter and enforced by the release audit; distinct from the coat's amplitude ceiling `|amp| <= P` (see **coat algebra**). ([Chapter 37](../papers/37-permission-map/paper.md) · [README](../README.md))

**cited** — Named and locked, but not copied into this public repository. Technical: an evidence state — a private artifact or chapter is referenced but not shipped for re-running here. ([how to read](how-to-read.md))

**coat** — The layer of phase a world can carry on top of its bare tally of outcomes — the part of its bookkeeping that lets contributions reinforce or cancel. Technical: the derived-phase amplitude structure over a fork census (phase built Feynman-checkerboard style from a corner count), present only over parented floors. ([Chapter 32](../papers/32-the-coat-and-the-count/paper.md) · [Chapter 41](../papers/41-fourth-column/paper.md))

**coat algebra** — The rulebook the phase layer obeys: amplitudes never beat the plain count, contributions either fully add or fully cancel, and each channel carries one pure phase. Technical: the generic-core triple of the coat — the ceiling `|amp| <= P`, the additivity dichotomy, and phase purity (the mod-4 law). ([Chapter 37](../papers/37-permission-map/paper.md) · [Chapter 32](../papers/32-the-coat-and-the-count/paper.md))

**completion** — An extra object bolted on beyond the finite staged content — a horizon or overlay the floor does not itself grow. Technical: an overlay or horizon object added beyond finite staged content. ([Chapter 56](../papers/56-fixed-point-and-receipts/paper.md))

**contact** — The founding event: a finite thing touches something larger than it can fully take in, leaving a difference that cannot be wiped away. Technical: a finite staged difference-relation, consumed once. ([Chapter 1](../papers/finite-contact-theory-v0.1.md))

**dark channel** — An outcome that is exactly forbidden because its contributions cancel perfectly. Technical: a census channel whose amplitudes sum to zero — exact destructive interference (see **preclusion**). ([Chapter 43](../papers/43-switch-lattice/paper.md) · [Chapter 37](../papers/37-permission-map/paper.md))

**equalized face** — The balanced, evened-out configuration a world can display but does not by itself pick. Technical: the uniform intermediate configuration a carrier can exhibit but does not select; a legitimate home for forced public content. ([public claim register](public-claim-register.md))

**extension** — A claim that goes beyond, or disagrees with, established physics or math, tested on its own terms rather than by whether it matches the textbook. Technical: a divergence claim split into an unconditional theorem and a conditional, experiment-open prediction, judged by internal rigor and a sharp observable difference — never by reduction to the incumbent. ([Chapter 10](../papers/10-negative-gram-holonomy/paper.md) · [public claim register](public-claim-register.md))

**finite reach** — Every difference the release talks about is built from finitely many steps. Technical: every staged difference in the release is reached by finitely many compositions. ([Chapter 1](../papers/finite-contact-theory-v0.1.md))

**floor** — The bare minimal rulebook a tiny universe is grown from — treated as more fundamental than any physics later read off it. Technical: the generative primitive (one-use contact under a tolerance relation with refinement) from which structure is grown and measured; rival floors are rival dynamics. ([Chapter 1](../papers/finite-contact-theory-v0.1.md) · [Chapter 37](../papers/37-permission-map/paper.md))

**fork** — A moment where several outcomes are set up as genuinely possible. Technical: a staged branching of possible continuations at a contact. ([Chapter 1](../papers/finite-contact-theory-v0.1.md))

**generic / contingent / instrument-indexed** — The three verdicts the program sorts every feature into: shows up in every world, special to some worlds, or dependent on how you measure. Technical: the taxonomy classes of the permission map — a law holding on all built floors, on some, or one whose verdict flips with the observable keying. ([Chapter 37](../papers/37-permission-map/paper.md))

**genealogy** — The record of which branch came from which — a fork's family tree. Technical: the branching-history and parentage structure of forks; the genealogy policy (e.g. collapsed vs. retained) is a received world input, not forced. ([Chapter 56](../papers/56-fixed-point-and-receipts/paper.md) · [Chapter 41](../papers/41-fourth-column/paper.md))

**given, the** — The ingredients a world must simply be handed, because its rules stage them but never pick them. Technical: the registry of received inputs, of two kinds — torsor-type (no privileged origin) and dial-type (forced origin, free magnitude). ([Chapter 29](../papers/29-two-kinds-of-the-given/paper.md))

**gluing** — The condition under which separate fragments can be received together as one. Technical: the co-reception condition that makes fragments jointly receivable. ([Chapter 8](../papers/08-nonexact-return/paper.md))

**graded defect** — A pass/fail check run one rung at a time up a finite ladder, used as a yes/no gate rather than a measured size. Technical: a sign/positivity condition checked grade by grade over a finite ladder; a legitimate carrier of forced public content alongside equalized faces. ([public claim register](public-claim-register.md))

**Gram discriminant (`Delta_3`)** — A single number that says whether three states could have come from ordinary quantum overlaps; going negative is the signature of the release's divergence. Technical: the determinant of the normalized three-state Gram matrix, `1 - r12^2 - r23^2 - r31^2 + 2 r12 r23 r31 cos Phi` — nonnegative for any Hilbert-space states, but negative in the Chapter-10 odd-holonomy sector. ([Chapter 10](../papers/10-negative-gram-holonomy/paper.md))

**held** — Not public-load-bearing yet. Technical: an evidence state — named but not yet shipped as public evidence. ([how to read](how-to-read.md))

**historical** — Kept for provenance, not to be read as current. Technical: an evidence state — records the path taken, not a public-load-bearing claim. ([how to read](how-to-read.md))

**holonomy** — The leftover twist a thing picks up when you carry it around a closed loop and bring it back. Technical: here, the `Z_2` identity phase a particle-identity transport acquires around a loop — trivial holonomy is pure gauge and cannot interfere; odd holonomy is the Chapter-10 divergence sector. ([Chapter 10](../papers/10-negative-gram-holonomy/paper.md) · [Chapter 43](../papers/43-switch-lattice/paper.md))

**mint** — For a world to actually produce, in its exact census, a phenomenon that another world forbids. Technical: to realize or exhibit an outcome class (a dark channel, a distance-4 correlation) in a floor's exact enumeration. ([Chapter 43](../papers/43-switch-lattice/paper.md) · [Chapter 37](../papers/37-permission-map/paper.md))

**no-signaling / monoidal composition** — Local moves can't send a signal, and combining parts is order-neutral and clean; which specific process fills that grammar is left open. Technical: the forced compositional grammar — local registration cannot signal, and composition is symmetric, associative, and unital — with the filling process theory not forced. ([Chapter 8](../papers/08-nonexact-return/paper.md))

**non-exact return** — When a finite contact that still matters cannot let a second use quietly undo the first; the return it owes is retained, not erased. Technical: the third-line primitive — a consequential contact retains a return its visible configuration has already completed, supplying the structure that forces the quaternionic cell. ([Chapter 8](../papers/08-nonexact-return/paper.md))

**occasion** — The single spent event a composition consumes (see **one-use**). Technical: the one-use event consumed by composition. ([Chapter 1](../papers/finite-contact-theory-v0.1.md))

**one-use** — The discipline that a contact is spent when used: the occasion is consumed and cannot be reused. Technical: primitive compositions consume their occasion and do not reuse it. ([Chapter 1](../papers/finite-contact-theory-v0.1.md))

**orientation / chirality** — Two of the handedness-like settings a world is simply handed: which way its structure is oriented, and a left-versus-right sign. Technical: two received world inputs — orientation, and the `±J` chirality sign that selects complex over real with no natural section. ([Chapter 56](../papers/56-fixed-point-and-receipts/paper.md))

**parent (of a fork)** — A reference branch that some outcomes can stay closer to than others — an anchor the branching is measured against. Technical: a distinguished/canonical ancestor branch; a fork is parented when it has one, and exact preclusion requires both a parent and a return. ([Chapter 41](../papers/41-fourth-column/paper.md) · [Chapter 43](../papers/43-switch-lattice/paper.md))

**Pell fence** — A whole-number sign test that decides which side of the quantum correlation limit a finite rung can land on. Technical: the negative-Pell relation `p^2 - 2q^2 = -1`, whose sign decides which side of the Tsirelson value `2*sqrt(2)` a finite rational rung sits on. ([Chapter 8](../papers/08-nonexact-return/paper.md))

**permission map** — The program's headline table: run the same measurements on rival worlds and read off which features are universal, which are special, and which depend on the instrument. Technical: the taxonomy artifact classifying each law generic / contingent / instrument-indexed across matched exact censuses of rival floors. ([Chapter 37](../papers/37-permission-map/paper.md))

**preclusion** — An outcome being exactly ruled out because its contributions cancel perfectly. Technical: exact destructive interference; the two-factor theorem shows it requires a dynamics that can *return* and a fork with a *parent*. ([Chapter 43](../papers/43-switch-lattice/paper.md))

**receipt** — One of the ingredients a world is handed rather than deriving — an entry stamped onto an occasion. Technical: a received (unforced) world input on the registry; Chapter 56 isolates five receipts that a complex quantum completion must supply. ([Chapter 56](../papers/56-fixed-point-and-receipts/paper.md))

**receiver** — Not a mind or a chooser, but the condition that lets contacts be taken in together. Technical: a gluing/co-reception condition; the retained interface forces the minimal receiver to be the quaternions with the `1+3` split. ([Chapter 8](../papers/08-nonexact-return/paper.md))

**recovery** — Re-deriving a known piece of physics or math from a floor that never assumed it — the program's main win condition, not a concession. Technical: standard structure reconstructed inside the finite-contact frame, labeled as recovery in the register. ([Chapter 8](../papers/08-nonexact-return/paper.md) · [README](../README.md))

**return** — A world's capacity to come back to a configuration it has left, so that contributions can meet again. Technical: the dynamical switch (free / impossible / priced) governing whether a class can recur — a precondition for interference. ([Chapter 43](../papers/43-switch-lattice/paper.md) · [Chapter 40](../papers/40-breathing-floor/paper.md))

**selector** — The extra ingredient that would pick one outcome out of the staged menu — which the rules pointedly never supply. Technical: any rule that would choose a single element from a staged fork; the floor forces none. ([Chapter 1](../papers/finite-contact-theory-v0.1.md))

**shipped** — Included and runnable in this public repository. Technical: an evidence state — reproducible from the shipped scripts here. ([how to read](how-to-read.md))

**stageability** — Whether a floor can even set up a given arena, which is weaker than being able to pick its weights. Technical: whether the floor can stage a given arena or configuration at a scope; staging an arena is weaker than selecting its weights. ([Chapter 37](../papers/37-permission-map/paper.md))

**"stages a fork and forces no selector"** — The central law: the rules set up a menu of possible outcomes, but nothing in the rules picks one — the pick is a separate ingredient the floor never supplies. Technical: the founding no-selector postulate, confirmed generic on every floor built here. ([Chapter 1](../papers/finite-contact-theory-v0.1.md) · [Chapter 37](../papers/37-permission-map/paper.md))

**switch lattice** — A map of possible worlds laid out on a grid of on/off design choices, where empty corners become worlds you can build and predict in advance. Technical: the lattice whose coordinates are the discovered dynamical switches (return, conservation, fork parentedness, record decomposability); its corners are rival dynamics. ([Chapter 43](../papers/43-switch-lattice/paper.md))

**totalization** — One still-open way the single actual outcome might be supplied — by closing over the whole rather than by a local pick. Technical: the program's standing open hinge for actuality, alongside the contextual, history-bearing, and explicitly-received alternatives. ([Chapter 8](../papers/08-nonexact-return/paper.md))

**trace class** — A bundle of serial presentations that count as the same once a chosen quotient is applied. Technical: an equivalence class of serial presentations under the quotient relevant to public counting. ([Chapter 12](../papers/12-count-regions/paper.md))

**triality sector** — One of three symmetric "thirds" of the finite object, whose three-way overlap is exactly where contextuality is born. Technical: one of the three `D_4` triality sectors of the state/receiver cell; any one or two admit a global noncontextual assignment, all three together are obstructed. ([Chapter 8](../papers/08-nonexact-return/paper.md))

**typicality residual** — The single-case wiggle room left over after averaging: probability is recovered up to this gap, not past it. Technical: the named single-case gap left after exchangeable counting concentrates. ([Chapter 6](../papers/06-measured-floor/paper.md))

**witness** — A finite item counted as evidence for a staged event. Technical: a finite fiber element counted by the admissible measure. ([Chapter 8](../papers/08-nonexact-return/paper.md))

**witness fiber** — The full finite bundle of witnesses sitting over one staged event, whose sizes become the counting weights. Technical: the finite set of witnesses over a staged event whose cardinalities are the counting weights. ([Chapter 8](../papers/08-nonexact-return/paper.md))

**world-phase** — The one overall phase a world is handed, like a global setting it cannot derive from inside itself. Technical: a conserved, received world input; which world-phase is selected is named open in the ceiling. ([Chapter 10](../papers/10-negative-gram-holonomy/paper.md) · [Chapter 29](../papers/29-two-kinds-of-the-given/paper.md))
